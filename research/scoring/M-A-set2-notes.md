## Judgement calls I found hard

1. **Chinese self-management.** DfE's main MFL document (which Chinese follows, save for the comprehension-language and literary-response exceptions in its annex) requires "research skills… initiate and conduct individual research" at A level (para 14). But Pearson's Chinese Paper 3 speaking-exam structure (Task 1 stimulus card 30 marks, Task 2 general discussion 42 marks) doesn't obviously carry the same discrete "individual research project" that AQA's French/Spanish/German Paper 3 does. I scored it 2 (consistent with the DfE requirement applying regardless of board) but flagged this as uncertain — a second scorer might reasonably score 1 if they read Pearson's implementation as not meeting the research-project bar.
2. **Criminology self-management: 2 vs 3.** Units 1 and 3 (internally assessed) sum to 50% of the qualification, which is at the rule-4 threshold for a 3 ("≥50% of the qualification is a self-directed extended project"). But WJEC's internal assessment uses structured, teacher-set "model assignments" with defined assessment criteria (AC1.1, AC1.2, etc.), which reads as more scaffolded coursework than Art & Design's or D&T's open-ended personal investigation/design-and-make brief. I scored 2, treating "self-directed" as doing real work in rule 4's examples (D&T, Art) — but a case for 3 exists on the percentage alone.
3. **Criminology scientific-method: 1 vs 2.** Unit 3 ("Crime Scene to Courtroom") requires analysing and assessing the usefulness of forensic/investigative techniques via realistic case-study scenarios — assessed, not just descriptive. But the skill definition is about *planning and carrying out* experiments, and Criminology students never do this; they study and evaluate existing forensic procedure. I scored 2 under rule 1 ("scores 2 only if the assessment regularly requires applying it to realistic cases"), but a stricter reading capping this at 1 (studying *about* forensic science, no practical execution at all) is defensible.
4. **Art & Design titles: practical-making at 3 for Graphic Communication and Photography.** The DfE content is identical (and generic) across all four titles I scored (Fine Art, Graphic Communication, Photography, Textile Design); only the "for example" media list differs by title. I initially scored Graphic Communication and Photography at 2 for practical-making (reasoning they are more software/tool-based than hands-on-materials), then revised to 3 to match the rubric's own "what a 3 usually looks like" table, which groups "Art titles" together as a practical-making 3 alongside Textiles. This means Photography and Graphic Communication now score 3 on *both* practical-making and content-production — technically a double-count of the same 100%-NEA practical work across two skills, which rule 6 warns against ("don't double-count one activity at 3 across near-neighbour skills without separate evidence"). I judged this acceptable here because the table explicitly licenses both, and the two skills are genuinely different constructs (physical/technical execution vs. producing finished media content), but it's a close call.
5. **English Language & Literature vs. English Language on `creativity`.** Both scored 2. English Language's evidence is a discrete NEA "Original Writing" task (part of the 20% NEA); English Lang & Lit's evidence is an AO-level requirement ("use English…creatively", producing texts for audiences) that isn't tied to one named component. I treated these as comparable in strength, but the Lang & Lit evidence is arguably softer (aim-level language, not a named assessed task), so a stricter scorer might drop it to 1.
6. **Music teamwork: 1 vs 2.** AQA Music 7272 explicitly *allows* ensemble performance (up to 8 musicians) within the Performance component but does not require it — a student could perform entirely solo. Rule 5 says teamwork scores 2+ "only when group work is assessed"; since ensemble is optional rather than guaranteed, I scored 1. This is a stricter reading than might be applied if "allowed and creditable" were treated as sufficient.
7. **Dance and Drama & Theatre `writing` at 2, not 3.** Both have a written-exam component that is ≥40-50% of the qualification with an explicit "extended writing skills" requirement (Dance Component 2, 50%; Drama Component 1, 40%). Rule 3's ≥20% NEA/practical threshold doesn't apply here since these are exam components, not NEA, but the size and explicitness of the writing requirement made 3 tempting. I kept these at 2 because the subjects' *core identity* (per rule 1's spirit and the subjects.ts descriptions) is physical performance/choreography, and writing is assessing knowledge *about* the practice rather than being the qualification's central skill — consistent with how the rubric's "what a 3 looks like" table reserves writing-3 for "English, History, the essay-based humanities."

## Rubric ambiguities I hit

1. **Rule 2 (numeracy) references "the DfE" setting minimum maths percentages, but for Geography the minimum quantitative-skills percentage (10%) comes from Ofqual's GCE subject-level conditions, not the DfE subject-content document itself.** I applied rule 2 as if this were DfE-equivalent, since the anchors (A2, A3) already do the same for science subjects via DfE Appendix 6, which is itself an Ofqual-adjacent instrument. Worth clarifying in the rubric whether "DfE" in rule 2 should read "DfE/Ofqual."
2. **No rule directly addresses when a topic within a specification is *optional* (one of several choices) versus compulsory.** I resolved this via §9 known-limitation 3 ("specs with options…are scored on the reference spec's compulsory elements") — for example, I scored Politics `sustainability` as 0 because "Ecologism" is only one of eight optional political-ideas topics, not a compulsory element, even though the DfE content lists it. A worked anchor showing how to handle an optional-topic case explicitly would help future scorers apply this consistently.
3. **The "what a 3 usually looks like" table is explicitly "a scorer's aid; it is not binding," but it directly conflicts with rule 6 in the Art & Design case above** (see hard call 4). It would help to state which takes precedence when the two pull in different directions.
4. **Rule 4 (self-management) doesn't say how to treat qualifications where the self-directed component is split across two units that individually fall under 50% but jointly reach it** (Criminology Units 1+3 = 50%), nor whether "self-directed" requires open-ended topic choice (as in Art/D&T) or is satisfied by an independently completed, teacher-set assignment (as in most applied/vocational internal assessment). This affected hard call 2.
