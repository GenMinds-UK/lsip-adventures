## Judgement calls

1. **Applied/vocational qualification sizing.** For each of the five BTEC-referenced subjects (Applied Science, Engineering, Health & Social Care, Construction & the Built Environment, Sport & Exercise Science) I used the **Extended Certificate** (360 GLH), which Pearson states is "equivalent in size to one A level" — matching §10 item 5's instruction. For Food Science & Nutrition I used the WJEC **Diploma** (3 units, 360 GLH), not the Certificate (1 unit, 180 GLH), for the same reason: the Diploma is the one-A-level-equivalent size. I scored only the qualification's *mandatory* units, per §9 limitation 3 ("specs with options are scored on the reference spec's compulsory elements") — e.g. Health & Social Care's Anatomy and Physiology unit and Food Science & Nutrition's "Experimenting to solve food production problems" unit are optional at this size, so I treated them as not guaranteed and scored `scientific-method` accordingly lower than the skill-hint table might imply.

2. **Environmental Science × `scientific-method` = 2, not 3.** The "what a 3 usually looks like" hint table lists "Environmental Science if practicals required" as a typical 3. But the DfE content explicitly states fieldwork/practical activities are "**not directly assessed**" (Appendix 1, indirect assessment only) — unlike biology/chemistry/physics, which have a distinct Appendix 5b of practical skills "identified for **direct assessment**" contributing to the Practical Endorsement. Since Rule 3 requires the skill to be "the explicit focus of an NEA or practical component… or of the Practical Endorsement" to be eligible for a 3, and Environmental Science has no such endorsement or directly-assessed practical component, I scored it 2 — the same treatment as the Psychology anchor (A6), which is explicitly capped at 2 for the identical reason ("they will not be directly assessed on these activities"). I flag this as a possible rubric ambiguity (see below) since it means the hint table and the literal rubric rules point in different directions for this cell.

3. **Engineering and Construction × `self-management` = 1, not 2.** Both qualifications' major design/product units (Engineering Unit 3; Construction Unit 2) are timed, supervised "set tasks" released to a case-study brief days in advance, completed within a fixed 8–12 hour supervised window — closer to a controlled exam than the open-ended, self-directed extended project envisioned by Rule 4 (and exemplified by D&T's 50% NEA). I scored these 1 (exam-like) rather than 2, and flagged it as a judgement call because they are still externally-assessed coursework-style tasks, which a more generous reading of Rule 4 could push to 2.

4. **"Studying about" vs "applying" (Rule 1) for the applied/vocational subjects.** Several BTEC/WJEC units ask learners to apply knowledge to realistic case studies within an internally-assessed report (e.g. HSC Unit 5's care-and-support case-study reports; Food Science & Nutrition Unit 1's "meeting nutritional needs of specific groups" scenarios). I treated this pattern as satisfying Rule 1's exception ("scores 2 only if the assessment regularly requires applying it to realistic cases") and scored HSC × `care-empathy` and Food Science & Nutrition × `care-empathy` at their full content-implied level (3 and 2 respectively) rather than capping at 2, since the case-study application is the entire assessed task, not an add-on.

5. **Further Mathematics × `data-analysis` = 1, not the naive extrapolation from Statistics.** Because Further Maths' applied content is a genuine option (mechanics / statistics / decision maths, at least one of which must reach ~10% but statistics is not guaranteed), I did not assume statistics content is present, per the "compulsory elements only" rule for specs with options (§9 limitation 3). This is more conservative than simply mirroring Mathematics' data-analysis score.

6. **Physical Education × `numeracy` = 1, despite an explicit DfE percentage.** The DfE content states data-analysis/quantitative skills are "a minimum of 5% of the overall A-level marks" — below the 10% floor Rule 2 sets for a numeracy score of 2. I scored numeracy 1 (used, no qualifying minimum) but scored PE's `data-analysis` at 2, since that 5% figure is explicitly about data-analysis-to-optimise-performance content, not general numeracy. This is a rubric edge case: Rule 2 is written for numeracy specifically, and a subject can have a *named, quantified* skill requirement that still falls under its threshold.

7. **Rule 6 (don't double-count) applied to lab/practical subjects.** For Biology, Chemistry, Physics and Applied Science I scored `practical-making` at 2 (not 3) even though `scientific-method` is 3, on the basis that "lab technique belongs in scientific-method" (the skill-hint table's own guidance) and rule 6's worked example (an experiment is `scientific-method` 3 but only `practical-making` 1–2).

8. **Engineering and Construction × `engineering` = 3.** Neither qualification is literally called "A level Engineering Science", but their mandatory content (Engineering Unit 1: applying forces/electrical/mechanical principles mathematically to solve engineering problems; Construction Unit 1: calculating how materials behave under forces/loads as structural members) matches the skill definition ("applying technical principles such as forces, electricity, materials and control systems to design, build, maintain or improve products and systems") more closely than any other subject in my set, so I scored both 3.

## Rubric ambiguities to flag to the adjudicator/methodology owner

- **Practical Endorsement vs "indirect assessment" for scientific-method (see judgement call 2).** The rubric's "what a 3 looks like" hint table and its own scoring Rule 3 appear to disagree for Environmental Science. Recommend either (a) confirming Environmental Science should be 2, and correcting the hint table, or (b) clarifying that a DfE-mandated minimum number of fieldwork/practical days counts as "the focus of… a practical component" under Rule 3 even without a formal Practical Endorsement badge.
- **Rule 4's "live assessed performance" vs a timed, supervised set task.** Rule 4 names two routes to a self-management score of 2: a substantial self-directed NEA (≥20%), or "a live assessed performance". BTEC/Pearson "set tasks" (Engineering Unit 3, Construction Unit 2) are timetabled, supervised, and released only days ahead — not self-directed, and not a "live performance" in the PE/Drama sense (they produce a written design proposal, not a live event). I scored these 1, but the rubric doesn't currently have clean language for this middle case (controlled coursework that isn't a live performance or an open NEA), and a different scorer could reasonably land on 2.
- **Rule 2's percentage bands are written for numeracy specifically**, but several specifications state a DfE/board-mandated percentage for a *related but distinct* skill (PE's 5% for data-analysis-to-optimise-performance; AQA Environmental Science's separately-stated 10% maths minimum). Recommend the methodology clarify whether Rule 2's exact bands should be imported wholesale for `data-analysis` too, or whether `data-analysis` should be scored on the general 0–3 rubric text only.
- **"One-A-level-equivalent" sizing for the applied/vocational subjects** is stated in §10 item 5 but the actual GLH-equivalence (BTEC Extended Certificate = 360 GLH; WJEC Diploma = 360 GLH; WJEC Certificate = 180 GLH) is not spelled out in the methodology. I inferred Diploma-not-Certificate for Food Science & Nutrition from Pearson's own "equivalent in size to one A level" language on the BTEC titles; worth confirming this was the intended reading, since a scorer picking the WJEC *Certificate* (1 unit only) would score very differently (no food-safety unit at all, so `law-ethics` would drop from 3 to 0–1).

## Hardest calls

1. **Environmental Science × `scientific-method`** — 2 vs 3 (see judgement call 2 above). This is the single cell I am least confident about, because the hint table and the literal rule text disagree.
2. **Engineering/Construction × `self-management`** — 1 vs 2, given the ambiguity between "supervised set task" and "live assessed performance" (judgement call 3).
3. **Food Science & Nutrition qualification size** (Certificate vs Diploma) — this affects several rows, most importantly `law-ethics` (3 if Diploma, since Unit 2 on food safety is only mandatory in the 3-unit Diploma; much lower if only the 1-unit Certificate, which is just Unit 1, is used).
4. **Engineering × `creativity`** — 2 vs 3. Unit 3 (Engineering Product Design and Manufacture) is squarely about iterative design, matching the D&T anchor's language closely, but it is a constrained, supervised 8-hour set task responding to a given case study rather than an open design brief, so I capped it at 2 rather than matching D&T's 3.
5. **Psychology × `data-analysis`** — 2 vs 3. Research methods (including inferential statistics) is compulsory, but I could not confirm from the DfE content alone what fraction of AQA 7182's marks this represents; I used 2 as a conservative estimate consistent with the skill-hint table's qualification ("Psychology research methods only if inferential statistics are central to the reference spec").
6. **Business Studies × `customer-service` and `law-ethics`** — both scored 2 on the basis of named-but-secondary content areas (marketing/customer needs; ethical-legal-environmental issues), which sit right at the boundary between Rule 1's "at most 2 unless applied to realistic cases" default and the possibility that AQA's case-study data response papers apply this content to realistic scenarios often enough to be scored the same as a 2 either way — so the call did not change the outcome, but the reasoning path was contestable.
