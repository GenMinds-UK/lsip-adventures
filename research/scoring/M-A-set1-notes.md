## Judgement calls I found hard

1. **Food Science & Nutrition qualification size.** The WJEC/Eduqas "Certificate" is only 180 GLH (half an A level); the "Diploma" (360 GLH: Unit 1 mandatory 180 GLH + Unit 2 mandatory 90 GLH + one 90 GLH optional unit) is the size that matches the other applied subjects in `subjects.ts`. I scored against the Diploma's two **mandatory** units only (Units 1-2, 75% of GLH) per Limitation 3 ("specs with options are scored on the reference spec's compulsory elements"), and did not assume Unit 3 ("Experimenting to solve food production problems") or Unit 4 is chosen. If Unit 3 is in fact near-universal in centres, `scientific-method` would likely rise from 1 to 2-3.
2. **Physical Education numeracy at 5%.** DfE sets numeracy at "a minimum of 5% of overall marks" — below rule 2's 10% floor for a score of 2, but a minimum *is* explicitly set (unlike a true "no minimum" case). I scored this 1, reading rule 2 literally (5% doesn't clear the 10% band), but this is a genuine edge case the rubric doesn't cover explicitly.
3. **Sport & Exercise Science leadership at Unit 6 (25% GLH).** Unit 6 "Coaching for Performance and Fitness" is a whole mandatory synoptic unit (≥20%) about planning/delivering/evaluating coaching — by the general ≥20% test for a 3 it could arguably reach 3. I capped it at 2 to stay consistent with the methodology's explicit "PE with a coaching role (2)" note in the "what a 3 usually looks like" table, on the view that coaching is fundamentally the same skill (leadership applied to a sporting context) whether examined (PE) or internally assessed (BTEC). Flagging this because it is a case where the weighting test and the worked-example table point in different directions.
4. **Engineering teamwork/leadership at Unit 2 (60/360 GLH = 16.7%).** "Delivery of Engineering Processes Safely as a Team" explicitly requires acting as "team member and team leader," a directly on-topic, wholly-dedicated unit, but at 16.7% it falls just under the ~20% marker for a 3. Scored 2 for both teamwork and leadership. A second scorer might reasonably read "the skill is what this whole unit is about" as sufficient for a 3 despite the sub-20% weighting.
5. **Construction & the Built Environment law-ethics at Unit 5 (60/360 GLH = 16.7%).** Same shape as (4): "Health and Safety in Construction" is a unit wholly and only about H&S legislation, just under 20% by weighting. Scored 2, consistent with (4) and with Construction not appearing in the "what a 3 usually looks like" row for `law-ethics` (only Law, Criminology, Health & Social Care are named there).
6. **Psychology critical-thinking.** AQA 7182's AO3 (analysis/evaluation) is roughly a third of all marks and is squarely about weighing evidence and reaching judgements about psychological theories/studies — a plausible case for 3. I scored 2, because (a) Psychology is not listed under the "typical 3" row for `critical-thinking` in the methodology's aid table (History, English Lit, Philosophy, Politics, Sociology, RS, Law, Geography NEA), and (b) `scientific-method` already captures the research-methods evaluation, and rule 6 warns against inflating a near-neighbour skill from the same evidence base without separate support. A second scorer weighing the AO3 percentage more heavily could reasonably score this 3.
7. **Accounting vs Economics vs Business on `commercial`.** The aid table groups these as "(2-3)" without saying which is which. I read the skill definition (customers, markets, marketing, sales, costs, financial information) as matching Business Studies most fully (scored 3, per anchor A14) and scored Economics and Accounting both 2, since Economics is market theory rather than applied business practice and Accounting is about financial records/reporting rather than markets/sales. A different split (e.g. Accounting at 3 for its exclusively financial focus) is defensible.
8. **Statistics vs Maths on `numeracy` and `problem-solving`.** Statistics' content is 100% mathematical, so I scored `numeracy` 3 as well as `data-analysis` 3 (rule 6 does not forbid two genuinely-warranted 3s from the same subject where the underlying assessed content differs in emphasis). But I did *not* extend `problem-solving` to 3 for Statistics, since the "what a 3 usually looks like" table's problem-solving row names Maths/Further Maths/CS/Physics/D&T/Engineering but not Statistics; Statistics scored 2 instead, on the view that "choosing the appropriate test" is closer to applied numeracy/data-analysis than to the general breaking-down-an-unfamiliar-problem skill the taxonomy intends.

## Rubric ambiguities hit

- **Rule 2's bands don't cover a stated minimum below 10%** (PE's 5%). The rubric jumps from "0 otherwise" / "1 if maths is used but no minimum is set" straight to "2 if 10-39%"; a stated-but-sub-10% minimum sits awkwardly between "no minimum" and "10%+".
- **The ~20% marks threshold for a 3** is written as "roughly ≥20%" for the general case, but for the applied/BTEC qualifications, mandatory units come in fixed 60/90/120 GLH blocks against a 360 GLH total, so the nearest weightings are 16.7%, 25% or 33%. Several units (Engineering's team-delivery unit, Construction's H&S unit) sit at exactly 16.7% — just below the line — which produced two of the hardest calls above (4, 5).
- **Whether a whole-unit-but-under-20%-weighted topic should default to 2 or be allowed to reach 3** because it is the *entire, undiluted* focus of that unit (as opposed to one strand within a bigger unit) is not addressed by rule 3, which is framed around NEA/practical components specifically, not named knowledge units generally. I resolved this by treating the general 0-3 rubric's "~20% of marks" test as the controlling one throughout, for consistency, rather than unit-level dedication.
- **Rule 1's cap for "studying about a skill" (leadership, in particular) interacts oddly with rule 7** for applied subjects: BTEC coaching/team-leadership units are internally assessed and require *doing*, not just knowing about, leadership — arguably clearing rule 1's "regularly requires applying it to realistic cases" bar for a 2, but the methodology's own worked example (PE coaching = 2) suggests the ceiling stays at 2 regardless. I followed the worked example (see hard call 3).
