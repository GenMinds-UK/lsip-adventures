## Area summary and key stats

- Population 1.57 million; 55,000+ businesses; £40bn GVA; 728,000+ jobs (2026 plan p.8, quoting the Lancashire Growth Plan, September 2025).
- Employment rate 75.2% for Lancashire overall, ranging from Hyndburn (61.5%) and Blackpool (62.6%) to Wyre (84.4%) and Fylde (84.5%) (p.10).
- Economic inactivity rate 24.4%, 2.9 percentage points above the national average — over 175,000 residents (p.11).
- Job density just 77 jobs per 100 working-age residents in 2023 (back to 2014 levels), 10% below the national average (p.11).
- 50% of vacancies require Level 2 qualifications or below; by 2050, Level 4+ qualifications are projected for ~28.2% of jobs, with Level 1 and Level 2 roles falling to 10% and 35% respectively (p.11).
- 61.5% of Lancashire residents are working age (16–64) vs 62.9% England & Wales; 20.1% aged 65+ vs 18.6% nationally (p.11).
- 16–24 claimant rate 6.2% vs national 5.6% (p.11). 8.3% of 16–17-year-olds in Blackpool were NEET in 2024 — among the highest rates in the North West (p.11).
- Aerospace cluster: world's fourth-largest, anchored by BAE Systems Samlesbury and Warton; supports 20,000 North West jobs and 900+ apprentices, with productivity 15% above the UK average (p.12, p.24).
- Advanced engineering & manufacturing contributes ~£6.4bn (18% of total GVA) (p.12).
- North West hosts the UK's second-largest cyber cluster: 300+ companies, ~12,000 professionals (p.12).
- Blackpool: UK's most-visited seaside resort, ~20 million visitors/year, £1.5bn visitor economy (Growth Plan) / £1.7bn GVA for the wider tourism sector including accommodation/food/arts/sports/recreation (p.12, p.20).
- Nuclear sector nationally: £16.1bn GVA, 211,500 jobs, GVA multiplier 2.6; North West share £3.9bn GVA, 50,000 jobs (p.25).

## Priorities (sector priorities from Part One)

The 2026 LSIP names its "LSIP Priority Sectors" (p.14) as Advanced Manufacturing & Engineering, Hospitality/Leisure & Tourism, Digital, National Security & Resilience and Clean Energy & Nuclear (aligned to the Lancashire Growth Plan and the national Industrial Strategy's growth-driving sectors), plus Construction and Social Care as two further sectors that "underpin the economy". These seven map to `priorities.csv`.

### Advanced Manufacturing & Engineering (pages 16–18, Annex A pp.35–36)

- Context: UK leader in aerospace, automotive and precision engineering. BAE Systems Warton/Samlesbury drives the Global Combat Air Programme. Manufacturing employed 85,500 people in 2024 and contributed £7.6bn GVA (current prices) in 2023 (p.16).
- Key stats: 63% of manufacturers recruit only to fill existing roles; only 1 in 4 will recruit for new positions in the next year; 41% may use apprenticeships; only 6% will consider employing someone directly from school/college (p.16).
- Skills gaps (verbatim):
  - "Within the more advanced manufacturing companies there is a significant increase in companies looking for digital skills, especially AI, data analysis, as well as supervisory, management and leadership skills." (p.17)
  - "the number of employers offering apprenticeships and technical education 'does not fulfil the interest from young people in Lancashire'" — Lancashire Careers Hub's Boosting Skills Project (p.17)
- Occupations named (SOC codes, level, status "Ongoing"): Engineers 2122/2126/2129 (L3-6); Machinists & production operatives incl. CNC 5223 (L3-4); Fabricators & welders 5211/5213 (L3-4); Electrical engineers 2123/3112 (L3); Data analysis 2133 (L3-6); Management 1121 (L4-6) (pp.16–17, Annex A p.35).
- Clusters/employers named: BAE Systems (Warton, Samlesbury); Lancashire & Cumbria Institute of Technology (IoT); Strategic Development Fund (SDF) and Local Skills Improvement Fund (LSIF)-funded providers (p.16-17).

### Construction (pages 18–19)

- Context: 50,000+ jobs in Lancashire. National forecast: 251,500 additional construction workers needed 2024–28, gap of ~1 million workers by 2032 nationally. £600m national construction skills package referenced (p.18).
- Key stats: 16–19 construction learners rose from 1,900 (2022/23) to 2,650 (2024/25) — a 39% rise (p.19). Home Builders Federation (national): 60% of FE construction students leave the industry shortly after finishing training (p.18).
- Skills gaps (verbatim):
  - "The local skills system cannot, of course, suddenly produce a ready and waiting workforce with a minimum of 5-10 years experience." (p.18)
  - "Feedback from employers points to a lack of preparedness and awareness of working conditions of those entering the workplace from education leading to early leavers from the sector." (p.19)
- Occupations: Trades — bricklaying 5313, plumbing 5315, carpentry & joinery 5316, roofing 5314 (Ongoing, L2-3); Electrical 5241 (Ongoing, L3); Management/Construction & Building Trades Supervisors 5330 (Ongoing, L3-6) (p.19, Annex A p.35).
- Clusters/employers: CITB (Construction Industry Training Board) intelligence used (Annex C p.49); Lancashire & Cumbria Institute of Technology.

### Hospitality, Leisure & Tourism (page 20)

- Context: 69,650 jobs in 2024, £1.7bn GVA (2023) for accommodation/food/arts/sports/recreation. Eden Project Morecambe forecast to create ~650 jobs (p.20).
- Skills gaps: "The recruitment and retention of chefs are the most frequently discussed issues by employer in the sector." (p.20). "few survey respondents expressed an interest in taking on apprentices or young people directly from school or college." (p.20)
- Occupations (Ongoing): Chefs 5434 (L2-4); Front of house 4216 (L2); Managerial 1221/1222/5436 (L3-4); General leisure/hospitality staff 6221/9265/9264 (L2) (p.20, Annex A p.36).
- Clusters/employers: Blackpool, Morecambe, Fylde coastal towns; Eden Project Morecambe.

### Social Care (page 21)

- Context: ~45,000 posts in 2024/25. North West needs a 22% rise in adult social care roles by 2040. Care-worker visa grants fell 84% in early 2024 (p.21).
- Key stats: 3,000+ existing vacancies in Lancashire. Skills for Care predicts an extra 83,665 staff needed across Lancashire and Cumbria by 2040. 52% of care workers across Lancashire and Cumbria haven't engaged with Care Certificate induction standards; 58% have no relevant qualification (p.21).
- Skills gaps (verbatim):
  - "Existing vacancies across Lancashire are already at over 3,000 with particular shortages in care workers and managers." (p.21)
  - "Other skills issues within the sector relate to difficulty in finding and arranging short course training for specific care skills." (p.21)
- Occupations: Care workers/home carers 6135 (L2-3); Senior care workers 6136 (L3); Nursing auxiliaries/assistants 6131 (L2-3); Public services associate professionals 3560 (L4-5); Quality assurance/regulatory professionals 2482 (L5-6); Health services/public health managers 1171 (L5-6); Social workers 2461 (L6) (Annex A pp.37–38).
- Clusters/employers: Skills for Care (national workforce intelligence body, cited throughout).

### Digital (pages 22–23)

- Context: Silicon Sands AI Growth Zone; Blackpool Enterprise Zone; National Cyber Force HQ at Samlesbury creating ~2,000 direct roles (potentially up to 15,000 with multiplier effects) (p.22).
- Skills gaps (verbatim):
  - "AI has become a standalone topic of discussion with nearly every employer interviewed in the research phase." (p.22)
  - "Difficulties for cyber graduates to into roles asking for experience even for junior roles." [sic] (p.23)
  - Future Dot Now's four Essential Digital Skills areas for AI: "1. AI Literacy and understanding; 2. Effective Communication with AI; 3. Critical evaluation of AI outputs; 4. Responsible and ethical use of AI" (pp.22–23)
- Most commonly requested skills: cyber security; web design, development and management; programming and coding; digital marketing, design and social media; plus Essential Digital Skills / MS Office for first-time entrants (p.22).
- Occupations: Programmers/software development 2134 (L4-6); Business analysts/architects/systems designers 2133 (L4-6); IT professionals n.e.c. 2139 (L5-6); IT Quality/Testing 2136 (L5-7); Web design/development 2141 (L3-5); AI implementation lead (emerging, no SOC code) (L6-7); IT managers 2132 (L6-7); IT network professionals 2137 (L4-6); IT user support technicians 3132 (L3-4) (Annex A pp.38–39).

## Cross-cutting themes (pages 15–16)

1. **Shortages in experienced staff** — employers prefer recruiting experienced staff over training newcomers; seen across manufacturing, construction and hospitality (p.15).
2. **Leadership & management** — training needed at supervisory, middle-management and leadership levels across sectors; apprenticeship de-funding is changing how this training is paid for (p.16).
3. **Digital skills & AI** — rising need across all sectors, handled in depth in the Digital section (p.16).
4. From the Executive Summary: youth unemployment, disengagement and rising NEET numbers (p.5-6); foundation/entry-level industries named as good starting points for young people: Retail, Hospitality, Leisure, Agriculture, Construction, Care, Administration (p.6).

## Employer survey findings

**2026 plan methodology** (Annex C, pp.48–51): over 500 organisations responded to survey work; 100+ one-to-one interviews held. Evidence triangulated from LMI (APS, BRES, job postings/Lightcast), employer surveys/interviews/forums, and skills-system/provider intelligence. Manufacturing-specific: 63% recruit only to fill existing roles; 1 in 4 recruiting for new positions; 41% may use apprenticeships; 6% would recruit directly from school/college (p.16).

**2023 plan Annex C — Lancashire Workforce & Skills survey, March 2023 (also referencing the Feb 2022 Skills survey and March 2022 Training Provision survey)** (pages 41–47 of `lancashire-lsip-2023.pdf`):
- Recruitment difficulties (p.41): difficulty finding right technical skills 49.1%; difficulty finding right behaviours/employability skills 42.8%; found people but not always meeting exact needs 39.6%; difficulty recruiting from local area 31.4%; difficulty finding sufficient numbers 30.8%; difficulty finding sufficient English/maths levels 6.9%; always found the right people easily 3.8%.
- Recruitment barriers (p.42): lack of workers with right experience level 37.1%; poor quality applications 32.1%; barriers to job offer take-up (pay, shifts, transport, caring) 27.0%; lack of appropriately qualified workers 24.5%; lack of good careers information/advice 22.6%; lack of work-ready school leavers 22.0%; lack of work-ready FE leavers 21.4%; lack of specific technical skills 18.9%; negative image of sector 18.2%; lack of work-ready HE leavers 13.8%.
- Awareness of training (p.43): good understanding of apprenticeships (have used) 69.7%; degree apprenticeships 34.9%; T-levels 11.8% (have used) / 23.7% (not yet used) but 30.3% "no understanding"; Skills Bootcamps 43.4% "no understanding".
- Training intended next 12 months (p.44): on-the-job training 74.0%; virtual/remote learning 44.8%; short modular courses 36.5%; apprenticeship 34.7%; degree apprenticeship 10.8%; Skills Bootcamps 4.3%.
- Most important candidate attribute (p.45): employability/interest/attitude 83.1%; experience 12.4%; qualifications/training 4.4%.
- Importance when recruiting (p.46): right attitude to work rated "essential" by 97.4% of employers — the highest of any attribute (vs literacy skills 22.5%, numeracy 21.0%, vocational/technical skills 21.0%, academic qualifications 13.0%).
- Soft skills trained (p.47): customer service 57.5%; problem-solving 54.5%; time management 53.3%; organisational 52.6%; verbal communication 51.5%; strong work ethic 49.5%; leadership 46.7%; working well in groups 45.8%; written communication 37.5%; adaptability 36.9%.

These 2023 figures pre-date the 2026 plan but the 2026 plan explicitly says "employer feedback continues many of the themes that were featured then" (p.4), so they remain useful for the plain-English "what employers say matters" framing, with a note that the underlying survey is from 2022/23.

## Top 5 skills gaps by strength of evidence

1. **The experience gap in recruitment.** This is the strongest, most consistently evidenced finding across the whole plan: 63% of manufacturers recruit only to fill existing roles, just 6% would hire straight from school or college, and the plan devotes its entire first main priority to it (2026 p.5, p.15-16). It shows up again almost word-for-word in construction and hospitality.
2. **Social care shortages.** Backed by hard numbers from Skills for Care: 3,000+ existing vacancies, 83,665 extra staff needed by 2040 across Lancashire and Cumbria, 52% of care workers not having done the Care Certificate induction and 58% with no relevant qualification (2026 p.21). This is precise, sourced, quantified evidence, unlike most other sectors' more qualitative "ongoing need" framing.
3. **Digital and AI skills, especially the AI implementation ladder.** Described as raised by "nearly every employer interviewed" (2026 p.22), with a clear five-stage maturity model and four named AI literacy areas from Future Dot Now research.
